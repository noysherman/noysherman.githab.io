01 / ABOUT

Hello, I’m Noy.

I am a master’s student in the laboratory of Prof. Amnon Bar-Shir at the Weizmann Institute of Science. My research area is molecular probes for magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

Working at the interface of chemistry and imaging, I am interested in how the properties of molecules can be used to obtain information about biological systems. MRI probes offer a way to connect molecular events with signals that can be detected non-invasively.

02 / RESEARCH

Making biology
visible.

From molecular design to an MRI-detectable signal.

MRI is widely used to visualize structures inside the body. Molecular imaging probes can add another layer of information, helping researchers investigate biological processes and the chemical environment within tissues.

The Bar-Shir Lab develops approaches to molecular and metabolic imaging using synthetic chemistry, magnetic resonance and synthetic biology. Its research includes synthetic MRI probes and MRI-compatible reporter systems.

Synthetic MRI probes

The group designs molecules that produce informative MRI readouts through mechanisms such as changes in water relaxation, proton exchange, or signals from nuclei including fluorine-19 and deuterium.

Molecular & metabolic imaging

The lab develops imaging strategies to study biological markers and metabolic processes in living systems, connecting molecular information with non-invasive detection.
